IN MEMORY OF
THE NANAS CUP
Nana — or GG, as her great-grandkids called her — was the heart of our family. With 6 kids, 16 grandkids, and 35 great-grandkids, her love stretched across generations. The house on Summer Street was always full of life. The girls loved sleepovers at Nana and Chick's, where there was always something waiting — a full candy drawer, a green bowl of soggy chips on top of the refrigerator, bowls of Rollos, and of course, the glass grapes that decorated her Living Room, a room that was rarely used. Sundays meant big family dinners, kids running around, playing on the swing set, or hiding in the house. You always knew where you stood with Nana — she never held back her opinions — but her honesty was always paired with humor, and she had a way of making even small things into family traditions. Even today, the glass grapes given to the Nana’s Cup golf champion each year come from Nana’s Living Room. They’re not just a prize, but a reminder of Nana and Chick, and of the laughter, love, and countless memories we shared in that house on Summer Street.
LONGEST DRIVE
Bud was the proud father of three, grandfather of four, and beloved husband of Ted. He was the family's “crazy uncle” — you never quite knew what to expect. In keeping with his father's tradition, his grandchildren lovingly called him Bud. He loved the outdoors, whether it was hiking, bird-watching, or fishing, and he had a soft spot for bulldogs. Sunday dinners at Nana and Chick's were a highlight, and Bud was known to keep everyone laughing — sometimes even opening the bathroom door mid-dinner to join in the kitchen table conversation. A one-time winner of the cherished “Nana's Cup” tournament, Bud celebrated in unforgettable style. At the family Christmas party, he displayed grapes like a museum piece and surrounded the trophy with red velvet ropes, turning a simple victory into a legendary memory. Today, part of the Nana's Cup tradition carries his legacy forward: a trophy cup is dedicated in Bud's memory, and because of his love of Mountain Dew, the winner of the “Longest Drive” must drink Mountain Dew rom the cup in his honor. Gone far too soon, Bud leaves behind laughter, love, and stories his family will treasure forever.
CLOSEST TO THE PIN
He was a devoted husband to Janet and a proud father of five, never missing a single concert, game, or family event. Known for his fear of missing out, he wanted to be part of every moment with those he loved. Family trips with extended family were always a highlight, filled with laughter thanks to his amazing sense of humor. To his grandchildren, he was simply “Jack,” a beloved grandpa who lit up every room. To his many nieces and nephews, he was the favorite uncle—always ready with a smile, a joke, or a helping hand. Jack especially loved participating in the annual Nana's Cup golf tournament. In his honor, the “Closest to the Pin” trophy now carries his spirit—complete with left-handed golf clubs, a perfect reflection of the way his friends and fellow golfers chose to remember him. Among his many talents was the remarkable ability to write both forward and backward at the same time. Though he never figured out how to turn that gift into a way to make money, his grandchildren loved watching him do it and delighted in his playful demonstrations. He often reminded his family of his love with his favorite saying: “You don't have to come with us, but we'd love you to.” We all miss him every day.
